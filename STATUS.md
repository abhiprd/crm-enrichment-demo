## M4 learning loop (live demo 2026-10-03/04 UTC, audited by `eval-auditor`)

Built: a rule store with append-only ruleset versions (`crm/rules.py`), a rule learner (`crm/learner.py`, gpt-6.1-sol at effort low) with code checks (one field, at most 300 characters, no names or organisations from the call, no duplicates), prompt composition of rules and reviewer-edit examples (`crm/extractor.py`), a validation gate (`crm/gate.py`), the reject-to-rule step run in the background from Slack (`crm/learning.py`), and `crm rules list | show | revert | validate`. Extractions and proposals record the ruleset version.

**Mechanism.** Two reviewer rejects on demo call A (competitors, `not_crm_worthy`; decision_timeline, `wrong_value` with a note) each produced one rule that passed the code checks. Each went into force immediately, flagged unvalidated, was gated on the 40 validation transcripts x 3 repeats with and without the rule, and was retired automatically by the gate (rule 1: v2 add, v3 retire; rule 2: v4 add, v5 retire). Rule 1 was later reinstated manually as v6 with `crm rules revert 2`; it is active and unvalidated. The reviewer notes contained call specifics; the rule texts did not.

**Gate results** (`results/gate_1.json`, `results/gate_2.json`). Gate 1 (rule 1, competitors): net +1 (3 improved, 2 worsened) against a minimum of 3 and a noise allowance of 9.0; other fields lost use_case -6 and champion -2; not passed. Gate 2 (rule 2, decision_timeline): net -1 (0 improved, 1 worsened); other fields lost competitors -4 and use_case -3; not passed. Each gate compared 40 transcripts x 3 repeats x 2 arms with 0 call errors. Both gates reused one cached empty-ruleset baseline, so they are not independent measurements and gate 2's incremental spend was about half of its reported cost.

**Observation on the gate (not a prediction).** The gate's "other fields may lose at most 2 net" limit is smaller than V0's measured run-to-run flip allowance on those fields (use_case 8.0, competitors 9.0 of 40, from `results/v0_noise.json`). In the two gates run, untouched fields moved by up to -6 (use_case) and -4 (competitors), which is inside that allowance. This suggests the gate is strict, possibly stricter than the measured noise justifies. Two rules are not enough to say how many it would retire. The gate counts majority-of-3 outcome changes while the allowance counts flips within one run, and it does not apply the paired-test criterion used for the M2 comparison. The thresholds were fixed in the plan before any rule was judged and were left unchanged by decision of the project owner.

**Paired demo** (`results/m4_demo.json`; demo data only, no noise floor, no statistics). Call A (Northwind Outfitters, Salesloft) and call B (Larkspur Wholesale, Outreach) contain the same trap: a vendor mentioned only as another company's tool. Rule 1 was written from a reject on call A. Under the empty ruleset (v5) the trap vendor was proposed as "evaluating" in 3 of 3 dry-run replays on each call. With rule 1 reinstated (v6) it was proposed in 0 of 3 on each, and Gong, a real evaluation on call B, was still proposed in 3 of 3. On call A the competitors proposal became empty, which means nothing new was proposed (the CRM already held Clari), not that a value was removed. A live Slack card for call B was also produced under v6 (one more run than the 3 replays). This shows that on two fictional calls the rule changed the extractor output in the intended direction on a call it was not written from. It does not show that the rule generalises.

**Exit criterion.** "Reject, then rule, then changed output on a paired transcript" is met mechanically and live. The learned rules did NOT clear the validation gate, so no improvement and no score gain from the learning loop is claimed. The demo shows the mechanism, not a validated gain.

Spend (`llm_calls`, this cycle): rule learner (Sol, 2 calls) about $0.003; gate replays (Luna, about 368 calls including retries) about $0.18; paired replays (Luna, 13 calls) about $0.007.

Other: the pipeline lets a junk stance value through (for example "not_applicable") when the extractor lists a vendor with no valid stance; the card then proposes it. Rule text is generated from the reviewer's note and the call; a name typed by a reviewer in a note is not checked against the rule text.

## M5 learning curve and ablation (audited by `eval-auditor`)

**Metric.** Proposal level (PLAN D1): did the extraction lead to the proposal in the key's `expected_proposal`. Truth-level scores of the same runs are in the same files. The gate's thresholds are unchanged; its outcome metric moved from truth to proposal level (M4's two gates were truth level). Chosen before the curve was run, but after the M2 truth-level results were seen.

**Baseline vs V0, re-scored post hoc (`results/proposal_scores.json`).** The comparison was pre-set at truth level, where it was within noise (26 changed vs 37 flipped of 360). At proposal level the baseline changed 26 of 360 instances (23 improved, 3 worsened) against 25 that flipped between V0's own runs. That clears the rule by 1 instance: treat it as borderline, not confirmation. Instances are clustered by 40 transcripts, so p is optimistic. The baseline prompt was tuned on the learn split against the truth-level score.

**Curve** (`results/curve.json`; validation, 40 transcripts, 3 repeats per point, 0 call errors; oracle reviewer through the 40 learn transcripts in 8 batches of 5, rules gated). The empty-ruleset prompt scored 0.8148 (3 runs; the 5-run reference is 0.8067). After batch 8 (3 gated rules, 13 reviewer-edit examples, `pv-ccfc7431`): 0.8898. House-rule instances 0.313 to 0.616 (33 instances); trap instances stayed near 1.0. Of 11 candidate rules, 3 passed the gate (champion, competitors, economic_buyer). Points 5 and 6 are one cached measurement. The batch-1 gain is mostly decision_timeline (quarter format), consistent with the few-shot examples, not the rules.

**Ablation** (`results/ablation.json`; 5 runs, paired against V0's saved runs, bar = 25 flipped of 360). V0 0.8067. Few-shot only 0.8428: 30 changed (22 improved, 8 worsened), clears by 5. Rules only 0.8483: 21 changed (19 improved, 2 worsened), within noise. Both 0.8906: 40 changed (37 improved, 3 worsened), clears. The ablation tests the final state, not individual rules.

**Truth level went down.** On the original metric the final state scored 0.851 vs V0's 0.866 (curve and ablation files): the gain is specific to the proposal-level metric and comes with lower truth-level fidelity, because house rules hide true values on purpose.

**Learner finding.** The oracle gives a reason code and no note. The learner declined to write a rule for all 6 budget and 2 next_step `not_crm_worthy` rejects it examined; `ballpark_budget` stayed 0 of 7 at every point and in every arm, and `dated_next_step` stayed at 0.2 to 0.27 (5 instances). Whether the cause is the missing note, the learner, or an unlearnable rule was not isolated.

**Caveats.** Validation was used to gate rules (rule 7 passed narrowly; rule 5 failed at the allowance), to draw the curve and for the ablation, so these are tuned-on numbers, not held-out. The test split was run once afterwards (next section). The oracle is idealized and supplies the key's convention when it edits, so this is what a perfect reviewer's feedback yields, not human review. Spend: curve $2.04, ablation $0.33 (both from their result files).

## M5 test run and hand review (audited by `eval-auditor`)

**Test run** (`results/test_run.json`; split = test, 20 transcripts x 9 fields = 180 instances, 5 repeats per arm, proposal-level metric). Run once, with the final version confirmed frozen by the project owner (300 calls, 3 arms x 20 transcripts x 5 repeats; final prompt `pv-ccfc7431`, matching `results/curve.json`; 0 call errors; $0.163). Mean score: V0 0.818, manual baseline 0.852, final 0.912. Noise bars are per pairing, from the first-named arm's own runs (V0: 15 of 180 instances flipped; baseline: 8 of 180), not one common bar; this differs from the V0-only bar in SPEC section 6. Final vs V0: 21 of 180 changed (19 improved, 2 worsened), p = 0.0002, clears. Final vs baseline: 18 of 180 changed (14 improved, 4 worsened), p = 0.031, clears (it also exceeds V0's bar of 15). Baseline vs V0: 11 of 180 changed (9 improved, 2 worsened), p = 0.065, within noise.

**Truth level reverses the order.** V0 0.879, baseline 0.926, final 0.850 (no paired test stored; say "lower", not "significantly lower"). The final arm was tuned toward the proposal metric (rules gated and examples drawn against `expected_proposal`); the baseline was tuned against truth on the learn split. So the final arm's gain does not show better extraction against the labels. The quarter_timeline house rule drives the timeline swing: proposal-level 0.99 (final) vs 0.60 (baseline); truth-level 0.61 vs 0.99. Group and case counts on 20 transcripts are tiny (house-rule instances 15; single cases 2 to 6). Single model, one 20-transcript split. The test split has been run; do not run it again.

**Hand review** (`results/handreview.json`): 10 validation calls, 58 proposals, all reviewed, one reviewer (the project owner), who knew the learn-split work and saw cards without keys. The final version produced the cards; clicks went to a stub, not HubSpot. The reviewer gave the same decision kind as the oracle on 45 of 58, and the same decision with matching detail on 43 of 58. The reviewer approved 9 proposals where the oracle wanted an edit (6) or a reject (3), and was stricter on 4 (3 edits, 1 reject). Edit-or-reject rates (not error rates against truth): reviewer 6 of 58, oracle 11 of 58. Disagreements by field: use_case 4 of 7, decision_timeline 3 of 6, next_step 2 of 5, pain_points 2 of 5, budget 1 of 7, champion 1 of 5; competitors, economic_buyer and stage_signal matched on all 23. After finishing, the reviewer reported that quarter proposals conflicted with exact transcript dates (the hidden quarter_timeline rule); that is self-reported and the file has no per-reason breakdown. This is a single-reviewer, small-n gap with no per-field significance. It shows the oracle is not a perfect stand-in for a human; it does not size that gap in general, and these validation calls are not held out. The listener for the Slack buttons stopped once (its 2-hour limit) and was restarted; clicks made while it was down did not register and were redone.
